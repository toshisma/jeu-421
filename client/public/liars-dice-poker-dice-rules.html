Liar’s dice rules with poker dice

Shake the cupA game of liar’s dice against the computer, right now · free

Five poker dice, an opaque cup and a straight face: you tell the next player what’s under the cup, and they decide whether to believe you. Here are the rules used on 421online (the French “poker menteur”), the hand rankings with their exact odds, and how to bluff better.

The game in 6 points

  1. You play with 5 poker dice and one cup, 2 to 6 players; everyone has 3 lives.
  2. The first player shakes the cup, looks at the dice in secret and calls a hand to the next player, true or not.
  3. The next player can believe: they look at the dice, re-roll 0 to 5 of them once, then call a strictly higher hand.
  4. Or they shout “Liar!”: the cup is lifted in front of everyone.
  5. If the dice are at least the call, the player who shouted loses a life; otherwise the caller does.
  6. A player with no lives left is out; the last one still in wins.

What you need

Five poker dice, whose faces run from lowest to highest: 9, 10, jack, queen, king, ace (910JQKA). You also need an opaque cup, so only the player holding it sees the dice. No chips and no score sheet: each player simply has a number of lives.

Comparing two hands

  • Nothing: compare the highest die, then the next one, and so on.
  • Pair: the pair’s value, then the other dice from high to low.
  • Two pair: the higher pair, then the lower one, then the odd die.
  • Three of a kind: its value, then the other two dice.
  • Full house: the three, then the pair (“kings over tens” beats “queens over aces”).
  • Four of a kind: its value, then the odd die. Five of a kind: its value.

Five aces (AAAAA) is the highest call in the game: nothing beats it.

How to call a hand

A call is a precise hand: the type and its values. You can say only what matters; any dice you don’t name count as the lowest possible. So plain “three jacks” means the lowest three jacks. Some calls, from lowest to highest:

  1. “Pair of queens” (meaning: with 9, 10 and jack)
  2. “Pair of queens, ace, 10, 9”: same pair, but an ace beside it, so higher
  3. “Two pair, jacks and nines”
  4. “Two pair, kings and tens”
  5. “Three nines”, then “three aces”
  6. “Low straight”, then “high straight”
  7. “Nines over tens”, “nines over aces”, then “tens over nines” (full houses)
  8. “Four tens”, then “five nines”… up to “five aces”

On 421online, the game suggests the hand just above the one you received, and a picker (hand type, then values) lets you call higher without typing anything.

A round, step by step

  1. The first player shakes the cup and looks at the five dice without showing them. On the first call of a round, any hand may be called.
  2. The next player chooses: “Liar!” or “I believe you”.
  3. If they believe, they take the cup and see the dice. They may re-roll once, 0 to 5 dice of their choice; the dice they keep stay put. Then they call a hand strictly higher than the one they received, true or not, to the next player.
  4. If they shout “Liar!”, the cup is lifted in front of everyone. Real hand at least equal to the call: the player who shouted loses a life. Lower: the caller loses a life.
  5. New round: the loser starts, with a fresh roll of all five dice.

If the call you receive is “five aces”, there’s nothing higher: all you can do is shout “Liar!”.

Worked example

Alice, Ben and Chloe have 3 lives each. Alice shakes the cup and sees KKJ109: a pair of kings. She calls, truthfully, “pair of kings”.

Ben believes her. He takes the cup, keeps the two kings and re-rolls the other three dice: KK99Q, two pair, kings and nines. He must call higher than “pair of kings”; he could tell the truth, but he’d rather pile on the pressure: “three tens”.

Chloe smells a bluff and shouts “Liar!”. The cup is lifted: two pair, lower than three tens. Ben lied, so he loses a life and starts the next round with a fresh roll. Had the cup held three tens, even just barely, Chloe would have lost the life.

Lives and the end of the game

Everyone starts with 3 lives (5 if you choose it before a private game or a game against the computer). A player with no lives left is out, and keeps their place in the ranking by order of elimination. The last player still in wins. Online, each player has 30 seconds to choose, re-roll and call; after that, the game believes for them, re-rolls nothing and calls the next hand up.

No-straights variant

Some tables don’t count straights. With the “no straights” setting (off by default), low and high straights disappear: 9-10-J-Q-K or 10-J-Q-K-A then count as “nothing”, which happens 9.3% of the time in one roll.

Bluffing tips

  • A pair is always believable: one roll gives at least a pair 93.8% of the time. Shouting “Liar!” at a small pair early in a round almost always loses.
  • Know what a re-roll can do: keeping a pair and re-rolling three dice gives at least three of a kind 44% of the time; keeping three of a kind and re-rolling two gives a full house or better 44% of the time; two pair with one die re-rolled makes a full house one time in three.
  • The higher the call, the more doubt pays: a full house or better comes up only 5.9% of the time in one roll. At that level, even with a re-roll, the caller has often had to make it up.
  • Call a little higher than you need to put pressure on the next player, who has to go higher still… but a call that’s too high draws “Liar!”.
  • Tell the truth now and then: a player who bluffs every time is easy to read. A real hand called with confidence costs lives to those who doubt too much.
  • Think one step ahead: when you believe, you’ll have to call higher. Believing a call that’s already very high means accepting you may have to bluff next.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best hand in this liar’s dice game?

Five aces: it’s the highest call in the game and nobody can beat it.

Does a straight beat three of a kind?

Yes: the low straight (9-10-J-Q-K) and the high straight (10-J-Q-K-A) beat every three of a kind, but lose to a full house, four of a kind and five of a kind.

What if the dice are exactly what was called?

Then the call was true: the player who shouted “Liar!” loses a life.

How many dice can I re-roll?

When you believe a call, you may re-roll once, 0 to 5 dice of your choice, before calling higher.

Do I have to tell the truth?

No, that’s the whole game: you can call a hand you don’t have, as long as it’s higher than the call you received.
