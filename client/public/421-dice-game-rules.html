How to play 421: the rules

Roll the diceA game of 421 against the computer, right now · free, no sign-up

421 is played with three dice and a pot of tokens. The aim is to get rid of all your tokens; the last player still holding some loses. These are the rules used on 421online by default, followed by the common variants.

The basics

You need three six-sided dice and 21 tokens (or matchsticks, or coins) in the middle of the table, for at least 2 players; 3 to 6 is ideal.

A round

Each player rolls the three dice on their turn. You can stop, or keep the dice you like and reroll the rest, up to 3 rolls in all. A kept die can still be rerolled on your next roll: nothing is final until you stop.

The first player of the round sets the limit: stop after one roll and everybody else gets one roll; use two and nobody gets more than two.

When everyone has played, the lowest hand loses the round, and the best hand sets the number of tokens at stake. The loser starts the next round.

The ranking of hands

From best to worst:

HandExampleValue
4-2-18 tokens
Three 1s7 tokens
Two 1s + x, then three x … x tokens (6 down to 2)
Straight 2 tokens
Any other hand 1 token
Nénette (the worst)the loser takes at least 2 tokens

Between two “other hands”, sort the dice from high to low and compare one by one: 6-5-2 beats 6-4-3. Two 1s beat three of a kind of the same value: 6-1-1 beats 6-6-6, which beats 5-1-1. The 2-2-1, called the nénette, is the worst hand in the game.

Loading

At the start, all 21 tokens are in the pot. Each round, the loser takes from the pot as many tokens as the best hand is worth. During the loading, the default here is a single roll each to keep things moving. When the pot is empty the loading ends, and anybody with no tokens has already won and leaves the game.

Unloading

The players holding tokens carry on, this time with three rolls. The player with the best hand gives the loser as many tokens as their hand is worth. When you have no tokens left, you’re out and safe. The last player holding tokens loses the game.

Worked example

Ties

  • Tied for last place: those players roll off, one roll each, and the lowest loses. Still tied? Roll again.
  • Tied for best hand during the unloading: a roll-off decides who gives the tokens.
  • A losing nénette always takes at least 2 tokens.
  • Near an empty pot the loser only takes what’s left, and in the unloading a winner only gives what they still have.

Variants

421 varies from one bar to the next, so agree before you start. On 421online, you can change the tokens, the number of rolls, the value of each hand, the tie rule, and the format: loading and unloading, unloading only (everyone starts with the same number of tokens), or points (a fixed number of rounds).

Frequently asked questions

How many dice do you use in 421?

Three, with up to three rolls per turn. The first player of each round sets how many rolls everyone else gets.

What is the best hand in 421?

4-2-1, worth 8 tokens by default. Next come three 1s, worth 7.

What is a nénette?

The 2-2-1, the worst hand of all. A player who loses with it takes at least 2 tokens.

Who loses at 421?

The last player still holding tokens at the end of the unloading. In French bars, that player traditionally buys the round.

How many tokens do you start with?

21 tokens are placed in the pot; the loading spreads them among the players.
